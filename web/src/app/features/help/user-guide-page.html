Getting started

A board is one piece of work — a revamp, an enhancement, a migration — owned by a squad. The application keeps those boards current and turns each one into a slide you can show without preparing anything.

Everything is reached from the dark rail on the left. Only destinations you can actually open are listed there, so the rail is shorter for a Viewer than for an administrator.

Saving, and what happens if two of you are editing

Typing changes the slide immediately but writes nothing. Save commits; Revert throws your edits away and returns to the stored version. If somebody else saves the board while you have it open, the page quietly refetches — unless you have unsaved edits, which are never overwritten by somebody else's save. A Live badge above the slide means that connection is working.

Worth a look

The amber panel is advisory, never blocking. It appears when a board says something a reviewer will immediately question — Blocked with no blocker note, a Medium-or-worse risk with no risk note, a squad with no Product Owner. You can save through all of it.

Change history

Every board keeps a change log: what changed, from what to what, when and by whom. Open it from History in the editor. It is also what the week-by-week analytics chart is reconstructed from.

Squad and roster

People exist once, on the roster, and are then placed onto boards. That is what makes "who is on three squads at once" a question the application can answer.

  1. 1
    Add somebody to a board

    In the board editor, start typing in Add squad member. Matching people appear as you type — pick one. A name nobody matches is created on the roster as you add them, so it is reusable next time rather than retyped.

  2. 2
    Give them a role

    Tech Lead, Developer, QA and so on. The role sets the colour of their card on the slide and feeds the squad-composition bar. Detail is the optional line underneath — a stack, a focus, "Angular · FHIR R4".

  3. 3
    Say how much of their week it takes

    On the Schedule, give the assignment an allocation and, if it really ends, a start and end date. Without an allocation the assignment counts as zero in the capacity maths — see below.

People in the rail is the roster itself: everyone's default role, default detail and whether they are still active. Retiring somebody there leaves their history on every board they worked on. Admin

Need a role that isn't listed?

Roles are configurable. An administrator adds one under Settings → Roles with its own colour, and it then appears everywhere a role is picked. The seven built-in roles cannot be deleted, only renamed and recoloured.

Tasks

Tasks on this board sits under the squad in the board editor. These are the things the work is actually waiting on — BRD sign-off, a UAT window, an ARB review, a data migration — not only what happens to be in Jira.

A task has a title, an optional assignee, a status, optional start and due dates, optional effort in hours, and an optional detail line. The assignee list is that board's own squad: add somebody to the squad first, then they can be given work.

  • Unassigned is a real state, not a gap to fill in. It is counted separately and it is the state a lead scans for when looking for something to hand out.
  • Status and assignee change straight from the row — the two edits people make constantly. Everything else goes through ✎.
  • Done stamps a completion date, and reopening a task clears it again, so "what did they finish last month" stays true.
  • Past due and not done shows as Overdue on the row, on the schedule and on the person's profile.
  • Completed tasks are hidden by default; the count under the list reveals them.

Viewers see the task list without the controls — reading who owes what is exactly a viewer's job.

Schedule and capacity

Schedule puts everyone down the side and weeks across the top. Each cell is free capacity — what that person still has available — because the question the page exists to answer is "who can take this on".

The number in the cell

Simple enough to explain in a sentence, because a number nobody can explain is a number nobody trusts:

available = 100 − whatever time off takes away that week

committed = the allocations on assignments running that week

free = available − committed  (negative means over-committed)

  • Red — committed beyond capacity.
  • Amber — fully away that week.
  • The footer totals free people-weeks across the team.

Two deliberate choices keep the total honest rather than flattering. An assignment with no allocation counts as zero, not as a guessed share — and the page says how many are in that state, so you know a total is understated rather than wrong. An assignment with no dates runs in every week, because "until further notice" is what most of them mean.

Time off

Only exceptions are recorded — leave, public holidays, training, part-time. Anything not listed is a normal full week. Record it from a person's profile with Record time off. Admin

The percentage you enter is what remains, not what is lost: 0 is fully away, 50 is half days. Where two periods overlap, the most restrictive one wins.

A person's profile

Click any name. You get their capacity this week, every assignment with its period and share, their recorded time off, their open and completed work, and the board changes they have made.

The activity feed matches on the display name the change log recorded, which is what it has stored since before there was a roster to point at. Two people sharing a name would merge, and a rename breaks the link — so the page prints how the match was made rather than presenting a partial history as if it were complete.

Programmes Admin

A programme is the layer above a board: VIDA 4, AI, and whatever comes next. Boards belong to one programme or to none.

  • Create and colour them under Settings → Categories.
  • Move boards in bulk from there, or one at a time with the Programme picker in the board editor.
  • Filter the portfolio by programme from the header, and every KPI recalculates for what is shown.
  • Retiring a programme never deletes a board. Its boards simply become uncategorised — which is a real, allowed state, not an error to clear.

Dashboard and analytics

Dashboard

The one screen to open before a meeting: counts by status, average progress by squad (lowest first — the squads to ask about), where the people are by role, the risk register, and what needs attention.

Analytics

Comparison rather than status. Seven views, all reading the same underlying data:

  • Progress week by week — the portfolio trend.
  • Squads compared over time — the same weeks, one line per squad.
  • Average completion by squad and boards per squad.
  • Delivery health by squad — the status mix inside each squad.
  • Capacity by squad and member workload — how much sits on each person.

About the week-by-week lines

History is reconstructed from the change log, so a chart can only go back as far as the record does. The page states how much real history it found instead of drawing a confident line through weeks it knows nothing about, and the line breaks at a gap rather than bridging it.

Presenting and exporting

Nothing has to be prepared for the meeting. The slides are the boards.

  1. 1
    Presentation Mode

    From the portfolio header, or Present in the board editor to open on that board. Filters carry over, so you can present one programme or one squad.

  2. 2
    Drive it from the keyboard

    →, space or Page Down for the next slide; ← or Page Up to go back; Home and End for the ends; Esc to leave.

  3. 3
    Take it away as a file

    PNG in the board editor captures exactly what is on screen. Export PDF on the portfolio renders the whole set server-side — available only where the host has a renderer installed, so it is hidden when it would not work.

Importing

  • A row with an Id updates that board. A row without one creates a new board.
  • An import replaces the portfolio with the file. If your file has no Members rows, squad membership goes with it — so export first and edit that export, rather than building a file from scratch.
  • A Category nobody has used yet is created during the import and reported back to you in the summary, so a typo shows up as a new programme rather than disappearing silently.
  • Anything the importer could not make sense of comes back as a warning listing the row, rather than failing the whole file.

Export JSON and Import JSON under "Also:" are the complete, lossless pair — ids, roster, membership and all. Use JSON when you are taking a backup before something risky; use Excel when a human has to read or edit it.

Jira and Smartsheet

A board can read its figures from a Jira project or a Smartsheet sheet instead of being typed in by hand.

Jira is {{ jiraEnabled() ? 'connected' : 'not connected' }}. Smartsheet is {{ smartsheetEnabled() ? 'connected' : 'not connected' }}. @if (!jiraEnabled() || !smartsheetEnabled()) { @if (isAdmin()) { Set one up under Jira or Smartsheet. } @else { An administrator sets these up. } }

The important design decision is the same for both: by default the application shows you what the source says and waits. Press Sync on a board, read the pulled figures beside the current ones, then Apply to draft and Save — two deliberate steps. An administrator can turn on automatic application instead, in which case a background job writes to every linked board on a schedule.

What sync will change

  • Sprint (Jira only — a sheet has no sprint)
  • Progress
  • Status

What it will never touch

  • Blocker note
  • Risk level and note
  • Squad members and roles
  • Title, product, programme

Both connections are read-only: the application never creates, edits, transitions or comments on anything in Jira or Smartsheet. Credentials are encrypted before they are stored and are never sent back to a browser — a saved token shows only its last four characters.

The full Jira guide covers setup, the exact status rules, the scheduled run and what to do when it does not work. Smartsheet works the same way, with a sheet id in place of a project key and two column names to tell it which columns hold progress and status.

Updating from Telegram

Send the bot a short message and your board updates itself. It is meant for the moment you actually know something — walking out of a meeting, on the way home — rather than the evening you finally sit down to update the board.

Connecting your account, once

A bot's name is public, so a message on its own proves nothing about who sent it. Get an enrolment code — your own from the Telegram screen, or from an administrator — then message the bot /start YOURCODE. That ties your Telegram account to your application account for good.

From then on the bot lets you do exactly what you can already do in the app. A Viewer still cannot change anything; a Product Owner can still only change their own boards. Codes expire after thirty minutes and work once.

The message

#update DISCHARGE
status: At Risk
progress: 65
sprint: Sprint 12
blocker: waiting on MOH sign-off
risk: Medium
note: UAT starts Monday
  • The first line names the board by its code. Codes are on the board editor, and /boards lists the ones you can update.
  • Only the lines you include change. Leave out progress and the progress stays as it was.
  • It is forgiving about wording — status: blocked, Status = Blocked, status - stuck and status: red all mean the same thing, and progress takes 65, 65% or 0.65.
  • A free note: becomes the blocker note when you are blocking the board, and the risk note when you are raising a risk.
  • It answers you with exactly what it changed, and says what it ignored.

Other things the bot understands:

/boards
The boards you can update, with their codes and current status.
/status CODE
What that board says right now.
/help
The template again.
/whoami
Which account you are posting as.

This one applies, it does not suggest

Unlike Jira and Smartsheet, a Telegram update is written to the board straight away. The difference is who is talking: those two are a machine inferring a status from issue counts, which deserves a human check, whereas this is you deliberately reporting your own — the same thing you would have typed into the editor. Every change is in the board's history, marked via Telegram and attributed to you.

An administrator can see every message the bot has been sent and what became of it, refusals included — which is where "my update never arrived" gets answered.

Accounts and passwords

Anyone can change their own password from Change password. Minimum twelve characters.

Administrators manage accounts under Settings → Users: create people, change a role, reset a password, deactivate somebody who has left. Admin

  • Changing a role, resetting a password or deactivating an account signs that person out everywhere immediately.
  • You cannot deactivate or demote yourself, and the last active administrator cannot be removed — locking everybody out of the application is not a mistake worth allowing.
  • An account is separate from a roster entry. Somebody can be on a squad without ever signing in, which is usually right for contributors who only need to appear on a slide.

When something looks wrong

The role dropdown is empty
Reference data did not load — usually the API is not running or your session expired. Reload the page; if the rail shows API unavailable, tell whoever runs the server.
Somebody's free capacity looks too high
Their assignments probably have no allocation, so they count as zero. The note under the schedule says how many are in that state. Set the allocations and the number becomes real.
A person shows no activity
The feed matches on display name. If they were renamed, or their roster name differs from the name on their account, the link breaks. The page says so rather than implying they did nothing.
A squad appears twice
Squads group on the text of the Squad field, so "Pradeep & Shehan" and "Pradeep and Shehan" are two squads. Correct the spelling on the board and they merge again.
Sync did nothing
Check the board has a project key or sheet id, and that the connection is set up at all — the status line at the top of this page says. The Jira guide has the full checklist.
An import undid something
Import replaces the portfolio with the file. Re-import the JSON export you took beforehand — which is why the advice above is to always export first.
